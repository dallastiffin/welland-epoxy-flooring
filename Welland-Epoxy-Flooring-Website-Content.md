## What The Ground Under Welland Does To A Floor

Welland sits on the Haldimand Clay Plain. The soil is dense lacustrine clay, it drains slowly, and the water table is high. After a wet spring, groundwater can stand within inches of the footings.

Clay holds that water instead of letting it run away. It presses against the underside of your slab and against the outside of your basement walls. Concrete is porous, so the water does not stop at the surface of the slab. It moves up through it as vapour.

That vapour has to go somewhere. On a bare slab it evaporates and nobody notices. Put a sealed coating on top and the vapour collects underneath it instead. Given enough pressure it lifts the coating off the concrete in sheets or bubbles.

This is called moisture vapour transmission, and it is the number one killer of resin floors in this part of Niagara. It has nothing to do with the quality of the resin. A premium product over a wet slab fails exactly like a cheap one.

## Why We Test The Slab Before We Quote It

We take a relative humidity reading from inside the concrete, not off the surface. A probe goes into a small hole drilled in the slab, it sits there while the hole equalises, and it tells us what the concrete is actually holding.

Surface tests do not do this. A plastic sheet taped down overnight tells you almost nothing on a Welland slab, because the surface can read dry while the body of the concrete is loaded with water.

If the reading is high, we do not walk away. We prime with a moisture tolerant system built to hold back vapour pressure, and we tell you in writing what the reading was and what we are doing about it.

If the reading is high and somebody quotes you without mentioning it, that is the quote to be careful with.

## Winter Here Is Wet, Not Just Cold

Welland does not get a deep freeze that holds all season. January averages a high of about minus one and a half and a low near minus seven. The slab spends the winter hovering right at the freezing point rather than sitting well below it.

That is worse for concrete than a hard freeze. Brine off the road melts on your garage floor, soaks into the pores, then refreezes overnight and expands. The next afternoon it melts again. Welland runs through that cycle over and over from December to March.

Each cycle pushes a little more of the surface apart. It shows up first as a pitted band inside the garage door where the car drips, then spreads back along the tire tracks.

Roughly fifty eight centimetres of snow falls here in an average year, across about sixty four days, with February usually the heaviest. That is not a lot of snow by Ontario standards. It is the melting and refreezing that does the damage, not the depth.

## What Are The Benefits Of Epoxy Flooring?

A coated slab stops drinking. Salt, oil, brake fluid and antifreeze sit on the surface until you wipe them off instead of soaking in and staining.

It stops the dust. Bare concrete sheds a fine grey powder for its whole life. Coated concrete does not, which matters if the space is attached to the house.

It is far easier to clean. A flake floor sweeps and mops. There is nowhere for grit to lodge.

It brightens a dark space. A basement or a single bay garage with one window gets noticeably lighter, because the coating reflects instead of absorbing.

It holds up to point loads. A jack stand, a floor jack or a pallet does not chip a properly built floor the way it chips bare concrete.

It gives you grip. Flake broadcast to refusal leaves texture underfoot, which counts on a slab that gets wet from a car in February.

## What Happens During Installation?

We grind first. Diamond grinders open the surface of the concrete so the resin can key into it mechanically. We do not acid etch. Etching leaves a residue and does not give a consistent profile, and it is behind a large share of the peeling floors we get called out to.

We cut out the cracks and fill them with a rigid repair resin, then grind that flat with the rest of the slab.

We take the moisture reading and choose the primer from it.

The base coat goes down. On a flake floor the chip is broadcast into the wet base until the floor will not take any more, which is what gives an even colour with no thin patches.

The following day the surplus chip is scraped back and vacuumed away, then the top coat is rolled. That layer is what stands up to salt, tires and sunlight.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a coating fix a damp basement floor?

No, and anyone who says otherwise is selling you a problem. A coating is not a waterproofing system. If water is coming in, that gets solved first. What we can do is measure how much vapour the slab is passing and build for it, or tell you plainly that the floor is not ready.

### My slab has cracks. Is it still worth coating?

Usually yes. Ordinary shrinkage cracks get routed out, filled with a rigid resin and ground flat, and they disappear into the finish. Cracks that are moving, or where one side sits higher than the other, are a different conversation and we will say so when we look.

### Can you coat in the middle of a Welland winter?

Polyaspartic can go down in cold conditions that epoxy cannot handle. What matters is the temperature of the slab itself, not the air, so we measure it and heat the space when it needs it. We would rather delay a week than lay resin on a slab that is too cold to cure properly.

### How soon can I park on it?

Foot traffic the same evening on most builds. Vehicles usually after about three days, sometimes sooner with a fast polyaspartic. Driving on a floor before the top coat has hardened is one of the few ways to damage a new floor, so we give you the dates in writing.

### Do you coat the floors in the newer subdivisions?

Yes, and quite a lot of them. The only thing to watch on a new build is the age of the concrete. Fresh slabs hold water for months after they are poured, so we take a reading and tell you whether it is ready or whether waiting a little longer will give you a better floor.

Add road salt and it gets worse, because brine keeps melting at temperatures where plain water would stay solid. That means more cycles, not fewer.

An attached garage has one more problem. The slab is often the coldest concrete in the house and it sits on the same wet clay as the basement, so it gets moisture from below as well as salt from above.

## The Water Table Is The Whole Problem

Welland sits low, on dense clay that drains slowly. Groundwater often stands close to the underside of the slab, and after snowmelt or a heavy spring it can be right up against it.

Concrete wicks that water upward. On a bare basement floor it evaporates into the room and you see it as a musty smell or a damp patch rather than standing water.

Seal the top with resin and the vapour has nowhere to go. It builds pressure under the coating and eventually pushes it off. You get blisters, or a sheet that lifts at an edge and keeps going.

Older basements make it likelier. Slabs poured before about 1960, which covers much of Dain City, Crowland and the older streets, generally have no vapour barrier underneath. The concrete is sitting directly on the clay.

## How We Deal With It

We drill a small hole and put a relative humidity probe inside the slab. It reads what the concrete holds, not what the surface feels like. Surface tests are close to useless here.

A low reading means a normal build.

A high reading means a moisture tolerant primer designed to hold back vapour pressure before anything else goes down.

A very high reading, or actual liquid water coming through, means the floor is not a coating problem yet. We will say so, explain what you are looking at, and we will not take money for a floor we expect to fail.

# Flake Epoxy Flooring In Welland, Ontario

## Flake Epoxy Flooring, Broadcast To Refusal

Flake epoxy is a resin base with coloured vinyl chip scattered into it while it is wet, sealed under a clear top coat. It is the finish most people picture when they think of a coated garage, and it is what we install most often across Welland.

The chip does real work. It hides marks, it puts texture underfoot, and it disguises the small imperfections every older slab has.

## Broadcast To Refusal Means Something

We scatter chip into the wet base until the floor will not accept any more. That is what refusal means, and it is the difference between a floor with even colour and one with thin patches that show as shadows in the light.

A partial broadcast uses less chip and costs less to install. It also leaves the base coat visible between the flakes, and it wears through sooner at the door line.

The following day the surplus is scraped back and vacuumed away. The clear coat then goes over the top and locks it all in.

## Why Texture Matters In This Climate

A Welland garage floor gets wet from December through March. Snow comes off the car, melts on the slab and sits there.

A smooth gloss floor is slippery when that happens. A full flake broadcast leaves a slightly raised profile that keeps grip when the floor is wet.

It is the same reason flake works well in basements with a laundry or a gym in them.

## Colour Choice

Blends are mixed from several chip colours, so you can go subtle or bold. Greys and charcoals are the common pick and hide the most.

Chip size changes the look. Larger chip reads as a bolder pattern, smaller chip closer to a stone finish.

Bring us a photo of the space and we will tell you what will look right in the light you have got.

## Where Flake Suits And Where It Does Not

It suits garages, basements, workshops, laundries and utility rooms.

## Polyaspartic Coating For Cold Slabs And Sunlight

Polyaspartic hardens quickly, tolerates slab temperatures that would leave epoxy soft, and keeps its colour in daylight. On a Welland garage, where the slab is cold half the year and the door is open to the sun, it is usually the right top coat.

## Cold Tolerance In Practice

Epoxy wants the slab above roughly ten degrees. Below that it slows down and can stop curing altogether, leaving a soft floor that never hardens properly.

Polyaspartic keeps curing far colder. That gives us a much longer working window in a climate where the garage slab sits near freezing from December onward.

What matters is the temperature of the concrete, not the air. We measure the slab, and if it needs heat before we start we bring heat.

## It Does Not Yellow

Clear epoxy goes yellow under ultraviolet light. In a garage with the door up through the summer, that shows within a season or two, and it shows worst in the strip of floor the sun reaches.

Polyaspartic holds its colour. That is why we specify it for garages, covered patios, porches and anywhere with real daylight.

## Fast Cure, Shorter Disruption

It hardens quickly enough that a floor can go from grinding to finished inside a day on some builds.

Fast cure cuts both ways. The working time is short, so it wants installers who have done it often. Rolled badly, polyaspartic shows every lap mark and there is no going back over it.

It resists hot tire pickup, which is what lifts cheaper coatings when a warm tire sits on them and cools.

It shrugs off salt, oil and brake fluid.

It takes a scuff more readily than a thick epoxy build, which is why we often use both. Epoxy underneath for thickness, polyaspartic on top for colour hold and wear.

Polyaspartic material costs more than epoxy. On a small floor the difference is modest and the colour hold is worth it. On a large indoor floor with no sunlight, a well built epoxy may be the better value and we will say so.

# Concrete Floor Coating In Welland, Ontario

## Concrete Coating That Starts With The Concrete

Coating concrete is mostly a preparation job. The resin is the easy part. What decides whether the floor lasts is what happens to the slab in the hours before anything is poured onto it.

We grind, we repair, we test, and only then do we coat. That order does not change.

## Grinding, Not Etching

Diamond grinding takes the closed surface off the concrete and opens the pores so resin can key into it. It gives a consistent profile across the whole floor, and it takes off old sealer, glue and laitance while it goes.

Acid etching is the shortcut. It uses acid to roughen the surface, and it leaves residue behind that has to be neutralised and rinsed. On a floor that is not perfectly flat, the acid pools and bites unevenly.

Most of the peeling floors we are called out to were etched rather than ground. We do not offer etching at all.

## Repairing What Is Already There

Shrinkage cracks get routed out into a clean channel, filled with a rigid resin and ground flat. Done properly they vanish under the finish.

Spalled and pitted areas, usually along a garage door line, get rebuilt with a patching compound and ground level.

Control joints are treated on purpose. Some get filled, some are honoured through the coating, and which one depends on whether the joint is still moving.

If a crack is active, or one side has dropped below the other, we will tell you. Coating over a moving crack means it reappears through the new floor.

## The Moisture Reading

On Welland slabs this is not optional. The clay under this city holds water against the concrete and the slab passes it upward as vapour.

We read the humidity inside the slab and pick the primer from the number. Where the reading is high we use a moisture tolerant system built to resist vapour pressure.

You get the reading in writing whether it is good news or bad.

## What A Plain Coated Slab Suits

A plain coloured build without flake works well in a workshop, a utility room, a plant floor or anywhere you want a smooth surface that is quick to clean.

It shows marks more than a flake floor does, so it is a better fit where the floor is not taking heavy daily traffic.

# Epoxy Floor Repair And Recoating In Welland, Ontario

## Fixing A Floor That Has Already Let Go

If your coating is peeling, bubbling or lifting at the edges, the resin is usually not what failed. Something underneath it did, and putting a fresh coat over the top will buy you a year at best.

We take failed floors off, work out why they went, and rebuild them so the same thing does not happen twice.

## Reading The Failure

The pattern tells the story, which is why we ask for a photo before we come out.

Bubbles and blisters across the middle of the floor, especially in a basement, generally mean moisture pushing up from the slab. That is the Welland classic and it points straight at the water table.

Sheets peeling cleanly away with the back of the coating smooth means the bond never formed. Almost always an etched floor rather than a ground one.

Circular patches lifting where the car sits is hot tire pickup, which comes from a coating too thin or too soft for the job. Retail kits do this.

Yellowing and chalking in the strip near the door is ultraviolet damage to a clear epoxy that should never have been used where the sun reaches.

Flaking along a crack means the crack was coated over rather than cut out and filled.

## Taking It Off

Old coating comes off with diamond grinding, and on stubborn floors with a shot blaster. It is dusty, noisy work and we run vacuum extraction throughout.

Recoating over a failing layer is not something we will do. The new floor can only hold as well as whatever it is stuck to, and a sound coating on top of a loose one still comes up.

Where the old coating is genuinely well bonded across the whole floor, an abrade and recoat is possible. We will test the bond in several places and tell you honestly which situation you are in.

## Rebuilding It Properly

Once the slab is bare we treat it as a new job. Grind, repair the cracks, take the humidity reading, prime to suit the number, then build the floor.

If the failure was moisture, the primer changes. That is the part the original installer usually skipped.

## What It Costs Compared To New

Stripping a failed floor adds to the price, because removal is labour. Expect a repair and rebuild to cost more than coating bare concrete would have in the first place.

That is worth knowing before you buy a cheap floor. The saving disappears the first time it has to come off.

# Patio And Outdoor Concrete Coating In Welland, Ontario

## Coating Outdoor Concrete In Niagara

A coated patio, porch or walkway seals the concrete against the water that would otherwise get into it and freeze. Outdoors is where Welland's freeze and thaw does its most visible damage, and an uncoated slab shows it within a few winters.

Outdoor work is a genuinely different build to an indoor floor, and it has a shorter season.

## Why Outdoor Slabs Break Up Here

Rain and snowmelt soak into the surface of the concrete. Overnight it freezes and expands. By afternoon it has thawed.

Welland crosses that line repeatedly through the winter rather than freezing solid and staying that way, so an outdoor slab gets far more cycles than the raw temperature suggests.

The result is scaling, where the top few millimetres flake away, and spalling around the edges and steps.

De-icing salt on a porch or a path accelerates all of it.

## What Outdoor Coatings Have To Do

They must resist ultraviolet light. Clear epoxy yellows outdoors quickly, so it does not go outside on our jobs. Polyaspartic and dedicated exterior systems hold their colour.

They must stay flexible enough to move with a slab that expands and contracts through a fifty degree annual swing.

They must give grip when wet. An outdoor surface with no texture is not usable in this climate, so aggregate goes into the top coat as standard.

They must let the slab breathe where it needs to, since an outdoor slab on Welland clay has water underneath it as well as on top.

## The Season Is Short

Outdoor coating needs settled conditions and a slab that is warm enough and dry enough. Realistically that is late spring through early autumn here.

# Metallic Epoxy Flooring In Welland, Ontario

## Metallic Epoxy For Floors People Look At

Metallic epoxy uses reflective pigment suspended in clear resin, worked while it is wet so the colour moves and pools. The result reads like polished stone or poured marble, and no two floors come out the same.

It is a decorative finish rather than a workhorse one, and it belongs where the floor is meant to be noticed.

## How The Effect Is Made

A solid base coat goes down first as the background.

The metallic layer follows, and while it is still wet it is moved with rollers, blades and air. That is what creates the veining and the depth.

A clear top coat seals it and gives the finished floor its gloss.

The working time is short and the effect cannot be corrected once it sets. It is a finish that depends entirely on the installer, which is worth knowing when you compare quotes on price alone.

## Where It Works

Bathrooms and ensuites, where it gives a stone look on a warm surface with no grout lines.

Entrances, feature walls and showrooms, where the floor is part of the impression.

Basement bars, games rooms and home cinemas.

Offices and salons where the floor is on display.

## Where We Would Steer You Elsewhere

A working garage. It shows tire marks and scuffs far more than flake, and repairing it invisibly is not straightforward.

Anywhere in direct sunlight, where the resin can yellow and the effect changes.

High traffic commercial floors, where flake or a plain build will look better for longer.

We would rather talk you out of it in the wrong room than install one you stop liking.

Silvers, charcoals, pearl whites, blues, coppers and bronzes are all available, usually blended.

## Maintenance

Sweep and damp mop. No harsh chemicals and nothing abrasive.

Recoating the clear layer some years down the line brings the gloss back without disturbing the metallic underneath.

# FAQ SECTION

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does Welland need slab moisture testing when other places seem not to bother?

Because of what this city is built on. Welland sits on the Haldimand Clay Plain, which is dense lacustrine clay that drains very slowly, and the water table sits high underneath it. After snowmelt or a wet spring, groundwater can stand within inches of the footings. Clay holds that water against the underside of your slab instead of letting it drain away, and concrete is porous enough to wick it upward and release it as vapour. On a bare floor nobody notices. Under a sealed coating that vapour collects and builds pressure until it pushes the floor off the concrete. What comes back off the probe decides the primer, and occasionally it decides that we should not be coating the floor yet at all. The check is quick and it is built into the quote.

### My house is in Dain City and the basement floor is old. Does that change anything?

It usually does. Slabs poured before roughly 1960, which covers a lot of Dain City, Crowland and the older streets, generally have no polyethylene vapour barrier underneath them. The concrete is sitting directly on the clay with nothing in between. Those floors read wet far more often than newer ones, and they are exactly the floors where a coating goes down over an untested slab and lifts eighteen months later. We coat plenty of them and they turn out well. We just find out what we are dealing with first.

### What does a coated floor cost here?

Professional systems in Canada generally run about seven to fifteen dollars a square foot fitted, with epoxy at the lower end and polyaspartic higher. Most installers work to a job minimum, because the grinder, the transport and the setup cost much the same whether the floor is small or large. In Welland the three things that move a price are the condition of the slab, the moisture reading, and whether there is an old coating that has to come off first. Those are market figures rather than a quote for your floor, so ring us and we will price the actual concrete.

### Is my garage floor pitting because of the cold?

It is pitting because of how often the temperature crosses freezing, which is a different thing. January in Welland averages a high around minus one and a half and a low near minus seven, so the slab hovers at the freezing point rather than sitting well below it. Salt water off the car soaks into the pores, freezes overnight, expands, then thaws the next afternoon. Repeat that through the winter and the surface breaks apart from the inside. Places that stay frozen solid all season get less of this, not more.

### Can you work in winter, or do I have to wait until spring?

Interior floors, usually yes. Polyaspartic keeps curing at temperatures where epoxy stops dead and stays soft. What we measure is the temperature of the slab, not the air on the wall thermometer, and where the concrete is too cold we bring heat in. Exterior work is different and genuinely seasonal. We will not lay a patio or porch coating in marginal conditions to meet a date, because it will fail in its first winter.

### How long before I can use the floor again?

Most garages and basements are two days on site with an overnight between coats. Foot traffic the evening of the second day is normal. Vehicles usually after about three days. Slab temperature and humidity both move those numbers, so we give you the real dates for your floor in writing before we leave rather than a rule of thumb.

### Are the kits from the hardware store any good?

Rarely, and the resin is not really the problem. A retail kit lays down a small fraction of the thickness of a professional build, and the instructions assume an etched slab rather than a ground one, so what little material there is never keys into the concrete properly. That is why hot tires lift them in patches. On a Welland slab there is a second problem, which is that the kit has no answer at all for moisture coming up from below. We strip a fair number of them out. If you are set on doing it yourself, at least hire a grinder.

### Can you coat a floor that already has a coating on it?

Sometimes. If the existing layer is genuinely well bonded across the whole floor, an abrade and recoat works and we will test the bond in several places to check. If any of it is lifting, it comes off, because a new floor can only hold as well as whatever is underneath it. Putting fresh resin over a failing layer buys a year at most and costs the same as doing it properly.
