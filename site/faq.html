Frequently Asked Questions

Because of what this city is built on. Welland sits on the Haldimand Clay Plain, which is dense lacustrine clay that drains very slowly, and the water table sits high underneath it. After snowmelt or a wet spring, groundwater can stand within inches of the footings. Clay holds that water against the underside of your slab instead of letting it drain away, and concrete is porous enough to wick it upward and release it as vapour. On a bare floor nobody notices. Under a sealed coating that vapour collects and builds pressure until it pushes the floor off the concrete. What comes back off the probe decides the primer, and occasionally it decides that we should not be coating the floor yet at all. The check is quick and it is built into the quote.

It usually does. Slabs poured before roughly 1960, which covers a lot of Dain City, Crowland and the older streets, generally have no polyethylene vapour barrier underneath them. The concrete is sitting directly on the clay with nothing in between. Those floors read wet far more often than newer ones, and they are exactly the floors where a coating goes down over an untested slab and lifts eighteen months later. We coat plenty of them and they turn out well. We just find out what we are dealing with first.

Professional systems in Canada generally run about seven to fifteen dollars a square foot fitted, with epoxy at the lower end and polyaspartic higher. Most installers work to a job minimum, because the grinder, the transport and the setup cost much the same whether the floor is small or large. In Welland the three things that move a price are the condition of the slab, the moisture reading, and whether there is an old coating that has to come off first. Those are market figures rather than a quote for your floor, so ring us and we will price the actual concrete.

It is pitting because of how often the temperature crosses freezing, which is a different thing. January in Welland averages a high around minus one and a half and a low near minus seven, so the slab hovers at the freezing point rather than sitting well below it. Salt water off the car soaks into the pores, freezes overnight, expands, then thaws the next afternoon. Repeat that through the winter and the surface breaks apart from the inside. Places that stay frozen solid all season get less of this, not more.

Interior floors, usually yes. Polyaspartic keeps curing at temperatures where epoxy stops dead and stays soft. What we measure is the temperature of the slab, not the air on the wall thermometer, and where the concrete is too cold we bring heat in. Exterior work is different and genuinely seasonal. We will not lay a patio or porch coating in marginal conditions to meet a date, because it will fail in its first winter.

Most garages and basements are two days on site with an overnight between coats. Foot traffic the evening of the second day is normal. Vehicles usually after about three days. Slab temperature and humidity both move those numbers, so we give you the real dates for your floor in writing before we leave rather than a rule of thumb.

Rarely, and the resin is not really the problem. A retail kit lays down a small fraction of the thickness of a professional build, and the instructions assume an etched slab rather than a ground one, so what little material there is never keys into the concrete properly. That is why hot tires lift them in patches. On a Welland slab there is a second problem, which is that the kit has no answer at all for moisture coming up from below. We strip a fair number of them out. If you are set on doing it yourself, at least hire a grinder.

Sometimes. If the existing layer is genuinely well bonded across the whole floor, an abrade and recoat works and we will test the bond in several places to check. If any of it is lifting, it comes off, because a new floor can only hold as well as whatever is underneath it. Putting fresh resin over a failing layer buys a year at most and costs the same as doing it properly.

What We Install

Choose A Floor Type

Not sure which one fits? Call and describe the space, and we will point you at the right build.

Garage Floor Coating

A coated garage floor stops the slab absorbing the brine your car carries in, and that is most of the battle in Welland. Bare concrete drinks salt water all winter, and the freeze and thaw that follows breaks the surface apart from the inside.

View Garage Floor Coating

Basement Floor Coating

A basement floor in Welland sits on wet clay with a high water table under it, which makes it the most demanding slab we coat in this city. Done properly it turns a cold grey box into a room you will actually use. Done without testing, it is the floor most likely to fail.

View Basement Floor Coating

Flake Epoxy Flooring

Flake epoxy is a resin base with coloured vinyl chip scattered into it while it is wet, sealed under a clear top coat. It is the finish most people picture when they think of a coated garage, and it is what we install most often across Welland.

View Flake Epoxy Flooring

Polyaspartic Floor Coating

Polyaspartic hardens quickly, tolerates slab temperatures that would leave epoxy soft, and keeps its colour in daylight. On a Welland garage, where the slab is cold half the year and the door is open to the sun, it is usually the right top coat.

View Polyaspartic Floor Coating

Concrete Floor Coating

Coating concrete is mostly a preparation job. The resin is the easy part. What decides whether the floor lasts is what happens to the slab in the hours before anything is poured onto it.

View Concrete Floor Coating

Epoxy Floor Repair and Recoating

If your coating is peeling, bubbling or lifting at the edges, the resin is usually not what failed. Something underneath it did, and putting a fresh coat over the top will buy you a year at best.

View Epoxy Floor Repair and Recoating

Commercial Epoxy Flooring

A commercial floor has to take trolleys, foot traffic, spills and cleaning chemicals, and it has to keep doing it without closing you down for a week. We build resin floors for shops, units, workshops and back of house areas across Welland and south Niagara.

View Commercial Epoxy Flooring

Industrial and Warehouse Flooring

Welland has been a manufacturing town since the canal was cut, and it is building again. There are six business parks in the city with a seventh in development, and the advanced manufacturing base has been growing rather than shrinking.

View Industrial and Warehouse Flooring

Patio and Outdoor Concrete Coating

A coated patio, porch or walkway seals the concrete against the water that would otherwise get into it and freeze. Outdoors is where Welland's freeze and thaw does its most visible damage, and an uncoated slab shows it within a few winters.

View Patio and Outdoor Concrete Coating

Metallic Epoxy Flooring

Metallic epoxy uses reflective pigment suspended in clear resin, worked while it is wet so the colour moves and pools. The result reads like polished stone or poured marble, and no two floors come out the same.
