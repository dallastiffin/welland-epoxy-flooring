Epoxy Flooring In Welland, Ontario

Welland sits on the Haldimand Clay Plain. The soil is dense lacustrine clay, it drains slowly, and the water table is high. After a wet spring, groundwater can stand within inches of the footings.

Clay holds that water instead of letting it run away. It presses against the underside of your slab and against the outside of your basement walls. Concrete is porous, so the water does not stop at the surface of the slab. It moves up through it as vapour.

That vapour has to go somewhere. On a bare slab it evaporates and nobody notices. Put a sealed coating on top and the vapour collects underneath it instead. Given enough pressure it lifts the coating off the concrete in sheets or bubbles.

This is called moisture vapour transmission, and it is the number one killer of resin floors in this part of Niagara. It has nothing to do with the quality of the resin. A premium product over a wet slab fails exactly like a cheap one.

Benefits

What Are The Benefits Of Epoxy Flooring?

✓

A coated slab stops drinking. Salt, oil, brake fluid and antifreeze sit on the surface until you wipe them off instead of soaking in and staining.

✓

It stops the dust. Bare concrete sheds a fine grey powder for its whole life. Coated concrete does not, which matters if the space is attached to the house.

✓

It is far easier to clean. A flake floor sweeps and mops. There is nowhere for grit to lodge.

✓

It brightens a dark space. A basement or a single bay garage with one window gets noticeably lighter, because the coating reflects instead of absorbing.

✓

It holds up to point loads. A jack stand, a floor jack or a pallet does not chip a properly built floor the way it chips bare concrete.

✓

It gives you grip. Flake broadcast to refusal leaves texture underfoot, which counts on a slab that gets wet from a car in February.

Why We Test The Slab Before We Quote It

We take a relative humidity reading from inside the concrete, not off the surface. A probe goes into a small hole drilled in the slab, it sits there while the hole equalises, and it tells us what the concrete is actually holding.

Surface tests do not do this. A plastic sheet taped down overnight tells you almost nothing on a Welland slab, because the surface can read dry while the body of the concrete is loaded with water.

If the reading is high, we do not walk away. We prime with a moisture tolerant system built to hold back vapour pressure, and we tell you in writing what the reading was and what we are doing about it.

If the reading is high and somebody quotes you without mentioning it, that is the quote to be careful with.

Winter Here Is Wet, Not Just Cold

Welland does not get a deep freeze that holds all season. January averages a high of about minus one and a half and a low near minus seven. The slab spends the winter hovering right at the freezing point rather than sitting well below it.

That is worse for concrete than a hard freeze. Brine off the road melts on your garage floor, soaks into the pores, then refreezes overnight and expands. The next afternoon it melts again. Welland runs through that cycle over and over from December to March.

Each cycle pushes a little more of the surface apart. It shows up first as a pitted band inside the garage door where the car drips, then spreads back along the tire tracks.

Roughly fifty eight centimetres of snow falls here in an average year, across about sixty four days, with February usually the heaviest. That is not a lot of snow by Ontario standards. It is the melting and refreezing that does the damage, not the depth.

On a slab like that, groundwater has an open path into the concrete. Those floors read wet far more often than newer ones do, and they are the floors where skipping the moisture test costs somebody a second installation.

We coat plenty of them successfully. We just do not guess at them.

How Long A Coated Floor Lasts Here

A properly built polyaspartic floor should give you fifteen to twenty years. Epoxy systems can run twenty or more indoors where there is no sunlight on them.

The resin is rarely what decides it. Preparation decides it. A floor that was ground properly, tested for moisture and primed to suit the reading will outlast a floor built from a better product over a slab nobody checked.

That is why our quotes describe the preparation in as much detail as the coating.

What A Coated Floor Costs In Welland

Professional systems in Canada generally land somewhere around seven to fifteen dollars a square foot fitted, depending on the build. Epoxy tends to sit at the lower end and polyaspartic higher.

Most installers, ourselves included, work to a job minimum. Grinding equipment, transport and setup cost roughly the same whether the floor is small or large.

Three things move a Welland price more than anything else: the state of the existing slab, the moisture reading, and whether there is an old coating to take off.

Those are figures for the market rather than a quote for your floor. Ring us and we will price the actual slab.

Epoxy Or Polyaspartic

Epoxy builds thickness and costs less. It is slower to cure and clear epoxy yellows in sunlight, so it belongs indoors.

Polyaspartic cures fast, holds its colour under sun, and tolerates cold application far better. It is what we reach for on garages and anywhere with daylight.

Plenty of our floors use both, with an epoxy base for build and a polyaspartic top for wear and colour hold.

Mistakes That Cost People A Second Floor

Coating over a slab that was never tested. On this clay, that is the big one.

Acid etching instead of grinding.

Going over an old coating that is already letting go. The new floor can only hold as well as the layer beneath it.

Kit thickness. A big box kit puts down a fraction of what a professional build does, which is why hot tires lift it.

Clear epoxy outside, which goes yellow within a season or two.

Our Process

What Happens During Installation?

  1. We grind first. Diamond grinders open the surface of the concrete so the resin can key into it mechanically. We do not acid etch. Etching leaves a residue and does not give a consistent profile, and it is behind a large share of the peeling floors we get called out to.

  2. We cut out the cracks and fill them with a rigid repair resin, then grind that flat with the rest of the slab.

  3. We take the moisture reading and choose the primer from it.

  4. The base coat goes down. On a flake floor the chip is broadcast into the wet base until the floor will not take any more, which is what gives an even colour with no thin patches.

  5. The following day the surplus chip is scraped back and vacuumed away, then the top coat is rolled. That layer is what stands up to salt, tires and sunlight.

  6. Most garages are two days on site. You can walk on it that evening and park on it after a few days, and we will give you the exact times for your floor before we leave.
